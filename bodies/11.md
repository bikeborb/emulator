### perf(vulkan): submit finished render passes while the frame is still being built

The host GPU only starts on what has been submitted to it. A frame was
submitted when the guest asked for a fence, every 1024 draws, or at its
end, so most of a frame reached the GPU after the guest had finished
building it. Games that wait for the GPU before they present (Tears of
the Kingdom and Super Mario Odyssey both do) then sat idle for the whole
render: with the speed limit out of the way, Tears of the Kingdom spent
34% of the time with the GPU thread and all three guest cores asleep.

When the guest moves to another render target and at least 96 draws have
been recorded since the last submission, the pass it leaves is submitted
at once. Only at a pass boundary, never inside a pass, so a tile-based
GPU does not pay a store and reload of the attachments.

The extra submissions cost the GPU thread time, and they cannot help
when that thread is itself what the frame waits for (Red Dead Redemption
at Armadillo, 4,700 draws a frame, measured 6% slower with them). So the
rule follows the share of time the GPU thread spends running, taken once
a frame: on below 80%, off above 90%, and after it has switched itself
off it asks for more idle time before trying again.

AYN Thor, speed limit at 1000 percent, one diagnostic build with the rule
switched off and on by a system property:
  Super Mario Odyssey  62.9 -> 71.9 fps (5 runs on: 70.7 to 72.7)
  Tears of the Kingdom 59.1 -> 65.9 fps (3 runs off, 5 runs on), time
                       with nobody working 34% -> 15%

With the automatic switch: Odyssey 73.0, Tears of the Kingdom 68.6, Red
Dead Redemption 45.4 against 46.3 with the rule forced off and 43.5 with
it forced on.

32 draws, and a timer of 0.4 ms or 1 ms in place of the draw count, gave
the same frame rate as 96 draws. Android only for now: it has not been
measured anywhere else.
